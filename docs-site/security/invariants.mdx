| # | Invariant | Enforced at | Pinned by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I1** | No consumer may treat an unrefreshed claim as valid indefinitely | `PassportCore::status_at`, `Passport::effective`, `statusAt` | `expiry_degrades_to_stale_not_valid` |
| **I2** | `STALE` is derived by the reader and **not issuable** | `Status` has no `Stale` variant; decoders reject other bytes | `UnknownStatus` paths |
| **I3** | `STALE ≠ INVALID`. Staleness is not a finding. | `Block::Stale` vs `Block::Invalid`; distinct program errors; amber vs red in the UI | Consumer error codes 6002 / 6003 |
| **I4** | **Absence is not permission** | `decide(Option<…>)` → `Block::Missing`; a required account on Solana; `NoPassport()` on EVM | `absence_is_not_permission`, `test_absenceIsNotPermission` |
| **I5** | A passport from the reader's future is rejected | `within_clock_bounds`, `IssuedInFuture` | `a_passport_from_the_future_is_rejected` |
| **I6** | Suppressing delivery can't hold a circuit open | Expiry → `Stale` → block, with no receipt-time expiry rejection | `test_blocksWhenNoUpdateEverArrives`; censorship drill (exactly 1 write) |

<Accordion title="In plain terms" icon="lightbulb">
These six rules must never be broken. Each one is written into the code in a specific place and checked by a specific automated test, so a future change that breaks one gets caught.

1. Labels must be renewed, or they stop counting.
2. Nobody can declare their own label "fresh".
3. "Out of date" and "problem found" are different things.
4. No label means stop.
5. Labels dated in the future are rejected.
6. Blocking messages can only make things stop, never keep them running.
</Accordion>

## Why I6 is the one that matters

Steps where a message *arrives* demonstrate the fast path. The safety property is what happens when **nothing arrives at all**. The relayer-censorship drill exists to show it on real infrastructure. A baseline passport, then silence, and the consumer blocked **41 seconds later on expiry alone**, after **exactly one** on-chain write.
